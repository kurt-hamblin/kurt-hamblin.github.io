Hello there.

I'm Kurt. I am unsure exactly what I wish to use this website for. I have had a website for years, but never really made anything of it. More than anything else, my idea now is to use this site to document personal projects, and also to put my rambling words onto a page to sort through my own thoughts and ideas.

I grew up in Silver Spring, Maryland, and received my B.S. in physics from the University of Maryland, Baltimore County (UMBC) in 2019, before moving to Lawrence, Kansas for graduate school. I spent seven years in Lawrence pursuing my PhD under [Dr. Allison Kirkpatrick](https://kirkpatrick.ku.edu/), studying the co-evolution of supermassive black holes and their host galaxies. During my PhD, I took a special interest in statistical methods, namely machine learning and Bayesian modeling, and ultimately firmly rooted myself as a Bayesian (at least, as firmly as a Bayesian can be about anything). I worked closely with JWST near-infrared and mid-infrared data, as well as X-ray data from Chandra. Notably, I developed a probabilistic machine learning framework for identifying active galactic nuclei in the distant universe from JWST NIRCam and MIRI photometry ([paper](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026ApJ..1002..223H/abstract), [code](https://github.com/kurt-hamblin/AGNBoost)).

It may seem silly to say, but doing a PhD is, well, hard. Those were certainly the seven hardest years of my life. I am, however, grateful for the experience and the people I met in my time at the University of Kansas. Although I have now left academia to work as a Physical Scientist at NOAA, I will always carry the experience and lessons of my PhD with me. Ultimately, it has taught me to be soft and kind, both to myself and others. Even though I have left the field, astronomy will always be an integral part of who I am and how I approach the world.

My path out of academia was motivated by nothing other than the realization that I enjoy science more generally than in the specific context of astronomy. I never quite shared the same passion for the field as my many brilliant colleagues, and it took me far too long to understand and accept that. I am, however, passionate about tackling and solving difficult problems. It may in fact be my biggest joy in life. The ability to be curious is, I think, an amazing facet of being human. I strive to stay curious in all parts of my life, as I find it both keeps things interesting and leads to unique ways of thinking about and solving problems.

Outside of work (and perhaps within it), I am, of course, a nerd. I love Star Wars (both watching and reading), Lord of the Rings, and World of Warcraft (which my partner tolerates, for some reason!). I enjoy being outdoors perhaps more than anything, and I unfortunately spend far too little time doing it. I was massively into road cycling in college, and while I don't cycle much anymore, I love pushing myself on a difficult hike. I also love archery, namely traditional Turkish archery, and I could (and will) happily talk anyone's ear off about it. I frequently find myself working on data projects related to my hobbies, some of which are on my [Projects](/projects/) page. I also write for fun under [Musings](/musings/).
