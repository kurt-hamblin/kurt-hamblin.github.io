I am a Physical Scientist at NOAA. I earned my PhD in Physics from the University of Kansas in July 2026, where I was advised by [Dr. Allison Kirkpatrick](https://kirkpatrick.ku.edu/) and was a NASA FINNEST Fellow. I defended with honors, and my thesis was titled "Uncovering Hidden Black Holes in the Distant Universe."
<!-- TODO: link the thesis title to ProQuest once it is available. -->

My PhD research focused on the co-evolution of supermassive black holes and their host galaxies. I was particularly interested in whether black hole mass assembly and star formation are truly coeval within individual galaxies. My last major project was AGNBoost, a probabilistic machine learning framework I developed to estimate photometric redshifts and identify AGN from JWST NIRCam and MIRI photometry ([paper](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026ApJ..1002..223H/abstract), [code](https://github.com/kurt-hamblin/AGNBoost)).

I am broadly interested in machine learning, particularly distributional and Bayesian methods that put solid constraints on prediction uncertainty. More recently, I have become interested in simulation-based inference.

I grew up in Silver Spring, Maryland, and received my B.S. in physics from the University of Maryland, Baltimore County (UMBC) in 2019, before moving to Lawrence, Kansas for graduate school.

Outside of work, I love Star Wars (watching and reading!), traditional archery, and performing my servant duties to my two pet rabbits, Yoda and Andromeda.
